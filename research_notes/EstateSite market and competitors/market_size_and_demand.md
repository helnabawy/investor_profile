# Market size and demand: personal website + AI lead capture for real-estate agents (Dubai first), as of November 2026

Reading notes for the writer:
- "Official" = Dubai Land Department (DLD), Dubai/Abu Dhabi Media Office, WAM, REGA, ADREC, Qatar Ministry of Justice. "Press" = The National, Khaleej Times, Gulf News, AGBI. "Vendor" = brokerages, proptech companies, chatbot sellers, blogs with something to sell.
- "(snippet only)" means the figure came from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened; treat as lower confidence and re-check before quoting.
- AED 3.6725 = USD 1.

## 1. How many brokers/agents and brokerage firms are there in Dubai, how many enter each year, and what is the churn?

### Takeaway
The official DLD count is 32,294 registered brokers and 9,785 brokerage offices at end-2025, with 13,083 new brokers registered during 2025 (+38% on 2024). Inflow is roughly 40% of the base per year, and recruiter evidence says average tenure has dropped to six months or less, but no source publishes an attrition rate or a "share closing zero deals" figure.

### Cited Findings
- OFFICIAL. Registered real-estate brokers at end-2025: 32,294. New brokers in 2025: 13,083, up 38% from 2024. Registered brokerage offices: 9,785. Published 9 March 2026. — [Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/march/09-03/dubai-brokerage-sector-strengthens-its-role-as-a-key-driver-of-market-growth-in-2025)
- OFFICIAL. Brokerage commissions in 2025: AED 13.59 billion, up 31% on 2024. Broker-executed transactions: 96,440, up 54%. Women in brokerage: 11,371; women's commissions AED 2.98 billion (+83%) on 28,909 transactions (+49%). — [Dubai Media Office, 9 Mar 2026](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/march/09-03/dubai-brokerage-sector-strengthens-its-role-as-a-key-driver-of-market-growth-in-2025)
- PRESS, conflicting. Khaleej Times (9 Feb 2026, citing DLD records) gives "about 39,776" active licensed brokers and commissions of at least AED 13.736 billion for 2025, and "over 7,900" brokerage firms. The same article is internally inconsistent on H1 2025 (32,978 registered brokers in one passage, "over 29,500" in another; 1,223 offices in one passage). H1 2025: 6,714 new entrants, commissions over AED 3.23 billion vs AED 1.62 billion in H1 2024, 42,181 broker transactions. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-property-brokers-rake-in-dh1373-billion-in-2025)
- PRESS / recruiter report (Executive Search, reported 30 Oct 2025): about 37 new agents enter the Dubai market per day; active agents "approaching 40,000"; more than 7,900 brokerages; hiring up 70% year on year. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries)
- Churn, same report: average job tenure fell from about 12 months to six months or less; rental and new agents often leave within three months; luxury and off-plan brokers stay up to two years; firms investing in training and mentorship report 80-90% retention; average deals per agent "fell sharply" (no figure). — [The National, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries)
- VENDOR / data site (snippet only, page returned 403): 33,873 licensed agents and 10,050 active brokerage firms as of May 2026, up from 5,268 agents and 1,367 firms in 2017; argues the sector is "shedding sole traders" and that 10-50 agent mid-tier firms with fixed costs are most exposed. — [DXBinteract](https://dxbinteract.com/news/dubai-brokerage-sector-shedding-sole-traders)
- VENDOR, outlier (snippet only): "over 24,000 active brokers across 4,500+ licensed brokerage firms as of Q1 2026". Conflicts with DLD; do not rely on it. — [Oliva blog](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/dubai-real-estate-brokerage-regulations-2026)
- VENDOR advice blog (snippet only, anecdotal): claims most new Dubai agents leave before finishing their first quarter. — [Betterhomes blog](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/becoming-an-agent/why-dubai-top-brokers-are-switching-agencies)

### Inferences
- New brokers in 2024 were roughly 9,500 (13,083 / 1.38). This is derived, not a published figure.
- 37 per day is about 13,500 a year, which matches the DLD's 13,083, so the recruiter figure and the official figure corroborate each other.
- The 32,294 vs ~39,776 gap is probably a definitional difference (registered broker cards at a date vs anyone who held a card or earned commission during the year). Use 32,294 as the conservative official base and "approaching 40,000" as the upper bound.
- 32,294 brokers across 9,785 offices is about 3.3 brokers per office, so the typical firm is very small. That fits the "independent agent or small brokerage" target.
- Rough churn signal: if mid-2025 stood at about 29,500-33,000 (Khaleej Times' two inconsistent figures) and 6,369 brokers were added in H2 2025 (13,083 minus 6,714) to reach 32,294, then several thousand brokers left the register in six months. The inputs are too inconsistent to state a rate; the direction (high exit alongside high entry) is clear.
- High entry plus short tenure means a subscription sold to individual agents faces high involuntary churn; the stable buyers are likely small brokerages and agents past their first year.

### Gaps
- No official end-2024 broker total was retrieved (a DLD "real estate services" release mentioning 29,577 brokers appeared in search results but the page did not load, so its date is unknown).
- No official end-2026 or mid-2026 DLD broker count; the only 2026 figure (33,873, May 2026) is from a data vendor and was not opened.
- No source gives the share of agents who close zero deals in a year, or an annual attrition rate. The widely repeated "most agents close nothing" claims were not found in any citable form.
- No regulator data on licence non-renewals or office closures in 2026.

## 2. How is the Dubai property market trending in 2025-2026, and what does a downturn imply for agent numbers and spend?

### Takeaway
2025 was a record (about AED 687 billion of sales across about 215,700 deals), but 2026 is a clear downturn: a regional war from late February 2026 cut nine-month transactions 23% and value 27%, Q3 value fell 47%, and prices are down 5-15% from end-2025. Off-plan has held up far better than resale and is now roughly 72-76% of deals, which favours agents selling developer inventory to investors.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 record: sales of about AED 686.8 billion across 215,741 transactions; primary sales 149,290 deals worth AED 448.1 billion (about 69% of deals), resales 66,451 deals worth AED 238.8 billion. Total real-estate activity including mortgages and other procedures exceeded AED 917 billion (DLD Director-General). — [Khaleej Times, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-property-brokers-rake-in-dh1373-billion-in-2025)
- Other outlets give AED 682.5 billion for 2025 sales (snippet only), so small discrepancies exist between trackers. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-property-market-closes-2025-with-record-dh6825-billion-in-sales-1.500396068)
- Q3 2026 (Cavendish Maxwell): sales value AED 72.6 billion, down 47% year on year; 34,000 transactions, down 38% (partly a registration lag); off-plan 65% of value and 72% of purchases. Nine months to September 2026: 112,580 transactions (down 23%), AED 292 billion (down 27%). — [The National, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/10/07/dubai-home-sale-transactions-fall-in-third-quarter-due-to-regional-uncertainty/)
- S&P Global Ratings (citing DLD): monthly average of 12,644 sales from March to September 2026, down 26% from 17,198 in January-February 2026; prices down 5% to 15% between end-2025 and September 2026; expects a "gradual" correction, apartments falling more than villas, luxury most exposed, and more secondary-market selling. — [The National, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/10/07/dubai-home-sale-transactions-fall-in-third-quarter-due-to-regional-uncertainty/)
- Cause: the Iran war began in late February 2026, with strikes on Gulf energy and civilian infrastructure; hospitality, tourism and aviation were also hit. Emaar founder Mohamed Alabbar described a 5-10% "adjustment" in Dubai real estate. — [The National, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/10/07/dubai-home-sale-transactions-fall-in-third-quarter-due-to-regional-uncertainty/)
- Q2 2026 (Betterhomes, vendor; snippet only): residential transactions 34,850 (down 31% year on year), value AED 84.9 billion (down 45%); secondary sales down 59% to 8,512; off-plan down 12% to 26,338, equal to 76% of activity. — [Betterhomes](https://www.bhomes.com/en/blog/market-reports/q2-2026-sales-cooled-to-aed849bn-but-prices-and-rents-kept-climbing)
- AGBI (press, snippet only) counted more than 38,000 residential transactions in Q2 2026 on DLD figures, so Q2 counts differ by tracker. — [AGBI, July 2026](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/real-estate/2026/07/dubai-property-sales-tumble-but-market-settling-at-true-value/)
- August 2026 (snippet only): house prices down 1.7% year on year, described as the first annual fall since 2021; off-plan above 75% of transactions. — [The Week, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/middle-east/2026/09/08/dubai-property-boom-over-august-2026-data-shows-prices-cooling.amp.html)
- Supply: more than 140,000 apartments and nearly 30,000 villas were under construction as of October 2025. — [The National, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries)
- Fitch (pre-war call, snippet only): a correction of up to about 15% expected from late 2025 into 2026; housing stock growing about 16% over 2025-2027 against about 5% population growth, assuming no delivery delays. — [Economy Middle East](https://economymiddleeast.com/news/dubai-real-estate-prices-to-drop-on-2026-supply-spike-says-fitch)
- Fortune, 1 June 2026 (snippet only): notes Fitch forecast a 15% correction before the conflict began; a secondary summary says Fitch now expects more than 15%, which was not confirmed against Fitch's own publication. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/property-prices-down-dubai/?rand=8593)
- Press framing of the effect on brokers (snippet only): a June 2026 piece describes a "survival test" in which buyers favour established names and advisory quality. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/brokers-face-survival-test-as-dubai-property-buyers-increasingly-look-for-quality-advice)

### Inferences
- At a constant roughly 2% effective commission rate (AED 13.59 billion on AED 686.8 billion in 2025), a 27% fall in sales value implies the 2026 commission pool is tracking around AED 10 billion annualised rather than 13.6 billion, spread over at least as many brokers. Per-agent income is therefore down materially in 2026.
- A downturn cuts both ways for EstateSite: fewer agents survive and discretionary spend tightens, but a low-priced tool that replaces expensive portal spend, and helps an agent look "established", fits the "flight to quality" narrative. Price sensitivity will be high; the low-cost positioning matters more now than in 2025.
- Off-plan resilience (72-76% of deals) means the surviving agent base is weighted towards off-plan sellers whose buyers are investors, which matches the product's AI concierge over developer inventory.
- Launch timing risk: the pitch is being made about nine months into the sharpest fall in volumes since 2020, with supply still arriving. Any agent-count-based market sizing should use a haircut on the end-2025 register.

### Gaps
- No Q3 2026 broker-commission figure and no 2026 count of brokers leaving. The effect of the downturn on agent numbers is inferred, not measured.
- Full-year 2026 forecasts from CBRE, Knight Frank, JLL, ValuStrat, UBS and Moody's were not retrieved.
- Delivery-pipeline unit counts for 2026-2027 (about 120,000 and 70,500) appeared only in an unverified blog and are left out of the findings.

## 3. How are agents paid, and what do they spend on marketing, software and portals?

### Takeaway
Dubai agents are mostly commission-driven, keeping 40-50% at large agencies and 60-70% at boutiques, with average commission income of about AED 18,000 a month in the 2025 boom. Hard data on Dubai agents' own marketing and software spend was not found; the only firm local price point is a CRM at AED 220 per user per month, and portal package prices are not published.

### Cited Findings
- Commission splits: 40-50% to the agent at large agencies, 60-70% at boutique agencies. Off-plan developer commissions 4-6%. Average per-broker commission about AED 18,000 a month; top luxury and off-plan performers above AED 1 million a year. (Executive Search report.) — [The National, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2025/10/30/dubai-real-estate-agents-salaries)
- Typical sale commission is a minimum of 2%, usually up to 5%. — [Khaleej Times, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-property-brokers-rake-in-dh1373-billion-in-2025)
- Bayut describes the typical agency-agent split as 50:50 (snippet only). — [Bayut agent portal](https://www.bayut.com/agentportal/demystifying-real-estate-commissions-in-dubai-who-pays-and-how-much/)
- Some firms pay a base salary of AED 5,000-20,000 a month (single vendor source, snippet only). — [Driven Properties](https://www.drivenproperties.com/blog/real-estate-agent-salary-in-dubai)
- Dubai CRM price point (vendor's own price, 31 Oct 2026): PropSpace brokerage CRM AED 220 per user per month excluding 5% VAT, minimum invoice AED 500 a month, 10% discount for one-year and 25% for two-year prepayment, no setup fee. Portal subscriptions are billed separately by the portals. — [PropSpace](https://www.propspace.com/blog/real-estate-crm-pricing-dubai)
- Portal costs (unofficial directory estimate, snippet only): premium or featured placements on Bayut and Property Finder run AED 500-5,000 per listing per month; marketing-agency retainers AED 5,000-50,000 a month. — [Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/business-directory/real-estate-marketing-agencies)
- US benchmark, NAR 2025 Technology Survey (1,241 responses, July 2025): 34% of agents spend USD 50-250 a month on technology, 20% spend USD 251-500, 24% spend over USD 500; 67% say their brokerage provides all the tech tools they need, yet they still spend their own money. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)

### Inferences
- AED 13.59 billion across 32,294 brokers is about AED 421,000 of gross commission per broker in 2025; at a 50% split that is about AED 17,500 a month, matching the recruiter's AED 18,000 figure. The mean hides a heavily skewed distribution (no distribution data found).
- In a commission-only model the agent, not the firm, often bears personal marketing costs, so the buyer of EstateSite is plausibly the individual. A price well below the AED 220/user CRM benchmark and far below a single featured portal listing is easy to position as cheap.
- If Dubai agents resemble US agents, most would tolerate USD 50-250 a month total tech spend; EstateSite would compete for a slice of that with CRM, portals and ad spend.

### Gaps
- No published Property Finder or Bayut rate card; packages are quoted privately by sales teams. The AED 500-5,000 per listing figure is an unofficial estimate.
- No survey of what Dubai agents personally spend per month on marketing or software.
- No data on who pays for portal credits in practice (agent vs brokerage) by firm type.

NAR's budget assumes 1.2 million. - United Kingdom (secondary write-ups of ONS data; snippet only): 25,665 active estate-agency businesses in 2025, up 2% from 25,155 in 2024. — [Property Industry Eye](https://propertyindustryeye.com/estate-agency-numbers-forecast-to-grow-4-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Dubai (about 32,000-40,000) plus Abu Dhabi (about 3,300) gives a UAE core of roughly 36,000-43,000 licensed agents. Adding Saudi Arabia takes the Gulf pool to roughly 120,000-160,000 depending on which REGA figure is current.
- Saudi licences include many part-time and individual "FAL" licence holders, so the count overstates full-time professionals compared with Dubai. Qatar is too small to matter as a standalone market.
- Egypt is large but informal; lack of licensing implies lower willingness and ability to pay and a harder sales motion.

### Gaps
- No Egypt broker count from any source. No end-2025 official Saudi total; the 117,691 figure's date needs confirming on REGA's site.
- UK figures are secondary summaries of ONS data and use inconsistent definitions (businesses vs people, with or without auctioneers).
- No figures retrieved for other Gulf markets (Sharjah, Ras Al Khaimah, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait).

## 5. Evidence on demand for personal agent websites

### Takeaway
The only solid survey evidence is from the US and it is lukewarm: just 12% of Realtors name their personal website as a top source of quality leads, against 39% for social media. No Dubai or MENA survey on agents' own websites was found, so local demand is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- NAR 2025 Technology Survey (July 2025, 1,241 usable responses from 49,233 invited): top sources of quality leads were social media 39%, CRM 23%, local MLS 17%, brokerage website 13%, digital ad campaigns 12%, personal business website 12%, listing portals 9%. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)
- Same survey: 68% of agents use AI; 46% use AI-generated content; 21% use a CRM with AI insights; only 7% use chatbots for lead capture or client communication. On AI's impact: 17% significant positive, 33% moderately positive, 46% no noticeable impact, 4% negative. — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)
- OLDER (2021 NAR Technology Survey; snippet only): 54% said their broker provides agent websites, 24% said agents pay for their own website, 18% had a page on the broker's site. — [NAR 2021 Technology Survey PDF](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/documents/2021-technology-survey-08-03-2021.pdf)
- Undated, about 2024 (snippet only): 45% of agents said their website was provided by their firm; nearly a quarter built and maintain their own. — [RealTrends](https://realtrends.com/articles/realtors-still-get-most-of-their-leads-from-facebook)
- Dubai context: commentators say buyers now favour established names and advisory quality over volume agents (snippet only). — [Khaleej Times, June 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/brokers-face-survival-test-as-dubai-property-buyers-increasingly-look-for-quality-advice)

### Inferences
- In the US roughly a quarter of agents pay for their own site, and a personal site is a top lead source for about one in eight. A realistic analogue for Dubai is that a minority of agents would pay, mostly established ones building a personal brand.
- The 7% chatbot adoption figure can be read two ways: the category is early and open, or agents have not found chatbots worth paying for. It is not evidence of pull.
- A personal website is not where most leads originate; social media is. The product is better pitched as the conversion destination for an agent's Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp traffic than as a lead source in its own right.
- Dubai differs from the US in that there is no MLS and portals (Property Finder, Bayut, Dubizzle) dominate discovery, so US percentages do not transfer directly.

### Gaps
- No Property Finder, Bayut or other MENA survey on the share of agents with their own website or the share of leads from own site vs portals vs social was found.
- No Dubai agent attitude survey on personal branding or willingness to pay for a site.
- The 2025 NAR survey's own-website ownership share was not reported in the coverage retrieved; the latest ownership split is from 2021.

## 6. Buyer behaviour: AI chat on agent sites, chatbot vs form conversion, response time, international buyers

### Takeaway
International and non-resident buyers are a large part of Dubai demand (roughly 41-42% of Q1 2026 transactions on blog readings of DLD data), which supports an always-on, cross-time-zone concierge. But the conversion case for chatbots rests almost entirely on vendor marketing, and no independent study separating chat format from response speed was found.

### Cited Findings
- International buyers (VENDOR blog citing DLD, snippet only): non-resident buyers were about 42% of Q1 2026 transactions, up from 40% in Q1 2025; top non-resident nationalities India 28%, UK 12%, Pakistan 9%, China 8%, Germany 6%. — [Oliva blog](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/dubai-foreign-buyer-share-q1-2026-2026)
- International buyers (VENDOR blog, snippet only, broader definition): international buyers crossed about 52% of Dubai residential transactions in early 2026. Not comparable with the 42% figure. — [Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/market-analysis/dubai-international-property-buyers-2026-nationalities-52-percent)
- Counter-signal: The National's headline on 2025 records attributes growth to more residents investing in new homes (snippet only), so resident end-users are also a major buyer group. — [The National, 13 Jan 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/01/13/dubai-property-transactions-hit-record-high-in-2025-as-more-residents-invest-in-new-homes)
- Response time (VENDOR post quoting a "Lead Connect" study, snippet only): responding within five minutes makes a company 21 times more likely to qualify a lead than waiting 30 minutes. — [Perspective AI blog](https://getperspective.ai/blog/conversational-ai-for-real-estate-why-top-agents-are-ditching-contact-forms)
- Response time baseline (VENDOR, snippet only, inconsistent): median real-estate lead response time of 15.3 hours attributed to "AgentZap"; other vendors cite about 40 minutes. — [Perspective AI blog](https://getperspective.ai/blog/real-estate-ai-chatbots-2026-replacing-contact-forms)
- Chatbot vs form (VENDOR's own data, snippet only): chatbot leads convert 12% higher than web-form leads. — [Realty AI](https://www.realty-ai.com/post/real-estate-ai-chatbot-statistics)
- Chatbot vs form (VENDOR anecdote, one Texas brokerage, snippet only): conversion rose from 14% to 39% within 90 days of replacing forms with AI chat. Not a controlled comparison. — [Perspective AI blog](https://getperspective.ai/blog/conversational-ai-for-real-estate-why-top-agents-are-ditching-contact-forms)
- Form baseline (VENDOR, snippet only): real-estate web forms convert at roughly 0.6%; a separate claim cites 81% form abandonment. — [Perspective AI blog](https://getperspective.ai/blog/real-estate-ai-chatbots-2026-replacing-contact-forms)
- Agent-side adoption: only 7% of US Realtors use chatbots for lead capture or client communication; 82% say clients respond positively to technology in the transaction. — [HousingWire on NAR 2025 survey](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-2025-technology-survey-realtor-tech-usage-trends/)

### Inferences
- Chatbot gains and speed-to-lead gains are confounded: a bot answers in seconds, so the uplift may come from speed, not conversation. The honest claim is "instant response to overseas buyers outside office hours", not "AI chat converts X times better".
- With top non-resident buyers in India, the UK, Pakistan, China and Germany, enquiries arrive across a spread of roughly eight time zones and several languages; a grounded multilingual concierge with WhatsApp handoff addresses a real operational gap for a solo agent.
- The 2026 war shock probably reduced the international share and raised buyer caution after Q1; the Q1 2026 figures predate most of the impact.

### Gaps
- No independent evidence that property buyers or investors want to use AI chatbots on agent sites; no buyer survey found.
- No official DLD primary source for the non-resident share; all figures are from commercial blogs.
- The commonly cited independent speed-to-lead research (Oldroyd et al., Harvard Business Review, 2011, which reported that firms contacting leads within an hour were about seven times likelier to qualify them) was not retrieved in this session; it is also 15 years old and not real-estate-specific. Verify before citing.
- No post-February-2026 data on how the international buyer share changed.

## 7. Market size estimates: real-estate software, proptech, CRM, chatbots

### Takeaway
Research-firm estimates are inconsistent to the point of being unusable as hard numbers: global real-estate software for 2025 ranges from about USD 4 billion to USD 13 billion, and MENA figures exist only for broad "proptech". A bottom-up agent-count calculation is more defensible than any top-down figure.

### Inferences
- "Proptech" totals include portals, mortgage, property management and construction tech, so they say little about agent websites or lead capture. Do not present them as the product's market.
- Illustrative bottom-up sizing (arithmetic on cited agent counts, with assumed prices that are NOT sourced): Dubai's 32,294 registered brokers at USD 30 a month would be about USD 11.6 million a year at 100% penetration; at 5% penetration about USD 0.6 million. Using 9,785 offices at USD 100 a month gives about USD 11.7 million at 100%. Adding Abu Dhabi and Saudi Arabia (about 120,000-160,000 agents in total) gives a theoretical ceiling of roughly USD 43-58 million a year at USD 30 a month. These are ceilings; realistic share is a low single-digit to low double-digit percentage.
- The 2026 downturn and six-month agent tenure both argue for discounting these ceilings and for modelling high monthly churn.

### Gaps
- No estimate specific to real-estate chatbots or real-estate CRM, globally or for MENA, was retrieved.
- No MENA-only real-estate software (as opposed to proptech-wide) figure exists in the sources found.
- No data on current revenue or customer counts of comparable agent-website vendors in the Gulf (covered, if at all, by the competitor research stream).
