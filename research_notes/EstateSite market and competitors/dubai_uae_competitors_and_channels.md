# Dubai / UAE / GCC competitors and lead channels for agent websites + AI lead tools (as of 11 Nov 2026)

Research note on source quality: almost every price and cost-per-lead figure below comes from vendor or agency marketing pages (who sell the thing they are pricing). No independent survey, regulator text, or audited benchmark was found for most figures. Each item is tagged with the source and its date; "UNVERIFIED" means secondary / self-interested source only. All retrieval was done 10-11 Nov 2026. No sign-ups or form submissions were made.

## 1. What local or region-focused products offer agent/brokerage websites or CRM+website bundles, and at what price?

### Takeaway
The UAE market is served by (a) broker CRMs priced per seat with a brokerage-level minimum (PropSpace is the only one with public pricing: AED 176-220/user/month, AED 500/month minimum), (b) off-plan inventory apps that are free or cheap for agents (Reelly, Alnair at AED 150/month, Behomes), (c) one-off web agencies charging AED 3,000-18,000 for a basic agent/broker site and AED 18,000-90,000+ for custom, and (d) generic WhatsApp/AI inbox vendors at USD 59-349/month. No product was found that bundles a premium personal agent site + off-plan project pages + inventory-grounded AI concierge as self-serve SaaS; the closest low-end item is knowme.ae (a free / AED 299 one-off agent profile page, no listings, no AI chat).

### Inferences
- Pricing anchors for EstateSite: the cheapest "site-like" local substitutes are free-AED 299 one-off (knowme.ae) and free-AED 150/month (inventory apps); the incumbent seat price for real broker software is ~AED 180-220/user/month with an AED 500 floor; a one-off agency site is AED 3,000-18,000. A self-serve SaaS in the AED 150-500/month band sits between inventory apps and agency builds; a self-hosted copy competes with the AED 6,000-40,000 agency one-off range.
- Broker CRMs sell to the brokerage (minimums, 12-month contracts), not to the individual agent; the individual-agent segment is addressed mainly by inventory apps and micro-page products.
- Generic WhatsApp/AI inbox tools (USD 59-349/month) are multi-seat team inboxes without a website or property data model; they compete with EstateSite's concierge only as a bolt-on to an existing site.
- EstateSite's missing pieces (portal/CRM integration, developer inventory feed) are exactly what the CRMs and inventory apps lead with, so agents may see it as an addition to, not a replacement for, PropSpace/Reelly-type tools.

- These apps substitute strongly for EstateSite's "off-plan project pages with payment plans and brochures" feature: they are free/cheap AND auto-populated from developer feeds, whereas EstateSite requires manual entry with no inventory feed. Manual project-page upkeep is the weakest part of EstateSite's proposition versus them.

## 4. How do Dubai agents acquire leads, what do they spend, and how important is a personal website?

### Takeaway
Portals (Property Finder, Bayut, Dubizzle) and referrals are consistently named as the primary sources, with Meta/Google ads to landing pages or click-to-WhatsApp as the main paid supplement; WhatsApp is the conversion layer for everything. Portal spend is quoted at roughly AED 2,500-15,000+ per portal per office per month (unofficial estimates; portals publish no price list); Meta CPLs cluster around AED 35-200. A personal website is described by agencies as an "owned funnel" and credibility asset, not as a primary lead source; no independent data quantifies its share.

### Cited Findings
- Five main channels listed: portals (PF, Bayut), referrals/networking, paid Google and Meta ads, organic content/social, and bought verified lead packs (updated 6 Oct 2026; publisher sells leads) — [Dubai Property Leads](https://dubaipropertyleads.ae/blog/how-real-estate-agents-get-leads-in-dubai)
- Portal subscription estimates per office per month: Property Finder AED 3,000-15,000+; Bayut AED 2,500-12,000+; both AED 6,000-25,000+; explicitly "planning estimates, not quotes"; drivers are listing volume, featured/premium placement, agent seats (15 Aug 2026; vendor sells AI lead response). UNVERIFIED — [Groovy Web](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/bayut-vs-property-finder-leads)
- "One brokerage spent around AED 90,000 a year on portal subscriptions"; portal subscriptions cost "tens of thousands of dirhams a year"; portal leads stop when payment stops (26 Jun 2026) — [Skimbox](https://www.skimbox.co/en/resources/blogs/real-estate-website-development-dubai)
- Neither portal publishes a package price list — [Tend, 2026](https://tend.ae/blog/property-finder-vs-bayut-vs-dubizzle-brokerages)
- Meta CPL claims (all agency sources): average AED 62 with WhatsApp funnels vs "industry average" AED 80-150; AED 35-80; AED 30-300 for lead-form leads; AED 50-200; a Dubai off-plan case study at AED 172 CPL with 116 leads and 2 bookings — [RedSpider](https://www.redspider.ae/blog/generate-real-estate-leads-using-google-meta-ads/), [Clozer CPL 2026](https://clozer.pages.dev/blog/cpl-uae-2026.html), [Propphy leads guide](https://www.propphy.com/blog/dubai-real-estate-leads-for-agents-and-brokers-2025), [BM Marketing](https://www.bmmarketing.ae/best-lead-generation-channels-real-estate-dubai/)
- Google Search: AED 450-900 per high-intent lead; high-intent property keyword CPCs up to AED 80-100 per click — [Dubai Property Leads](https://dubaipropertyleads.ae/blog/how-real-estate-agents-get-leads-in-dubai), [Clozer](https://clozer.pages.dev/blog/real-estate-lead-generation-dubai.html)
- Purchased lead packs: 10 leads for AED 1,500 trial; AED 115-150 per "verified" lead; leads generally AED 50-500 each — [Dubai Property Leads](https://dubaipropertyleads.ae/blog/how-real-estate-agents-get-leads-in-dubai)
- Lead-quality caution: a Dubai Marina brokerage Meta campaign produced ~180 leads/month at AED 180 per form fill; after adding qualifying questions it fell to 90 leads at AED 310 — [Clozer](https://clozer.pages.dev/blog/real-estate-agent-marketing-dubai.html)

- Conversion: portal leads ~0.4-1.2%, broader internet leads 2-3% (blog citing Real Trends / InsideSales; not Dubai-specific); a brokerage with 90% of leads from portals paying AED 250-400 per qualified lead — [Propphy](https://www.propphy.com/blog/how-dubai-real-estate-agents-generate-leads-online-2026)
- Time to results: portals 1-2 weeks, social media 12-18 months — [Dubai Fast Living](https://dubaifastliving.com/how-to-market-yourself-as-a-real-estate-agent-in-dubai/)
- Social media "mostly for visibility, trust, and retargeting rather than cold high-intent acquisition"; agents "controlling their own acquisition funnel" do better than portal-only (agency opinion) — [Propphy](https://www.propphy.com/blog/how-dubai-real-estate-agents-generate-leads-online-2026)
- Speed-to-lead: response within 5 minutes converts "up to 21x" better than after 30 minutes (HBR, cited by vendor; old non-Dubai study) — [Groovy Web](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/bayut-vs-property-finder-leads)
- Market size context: Dubai real estate exceeded AED 917 billion in 2025 across 270,000+ transactions (as cited by an agency blog) — [Skimbox](https://www.skimbox.co/en/resources/blogs/real-estate-website-development-dubai)
- Typical commission: 2% on resale = AED 20,000 on an AED 1,000,000 sale, plus VAT — [Property Finder blog](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/how-much-is-real-estate-commission-dubai/)

### Inferences
- Against portal spend of AED 30,000-180,000+/year per office and ad spend where a single qualified lead costs AED 150-400, a website subscription of a few hundred AED/month is small; the buying question for agents is "does it produce or convert leads", not affordability. One extra closed deal (AED 20,000+ commission) pays for years of subscription.
- A personal site generates few leads on its own; its realistic roles are (1) landing destination for Meta/Google/Instagram/TikTok traffic, (2) credibility check when a prospect Googles the agent, (3) 24/7 instant response + WhatsApp handoff (the speed-to-lead argument that AI-chat vendors use). EstateSite's concierge is best pitched on role 3 and on being the ad landing page, which implies needing ad-landing-page features (UTM/pixel tracking, per-project landing pages).
- Off-plan-focused agents (the EstateSite target) rely less on portals and more on ads, social and developer events than resale/rental agents — plausible but not evidenced by any source found.

### Gaps

## 5. Regulatory constraints: RERA/DLD advertising rules for an agent's own website, personal-brand vs brokerage, and UAE PDPL

### Takeaway
Property advertising in Dubai on any channel — including the agent's own website, social posts and WhatsApp marketing — requires a Trakheesi permit per advert, with the permit number/QR code, the brokerage's trade name and ORN and the agent's name and BRN displayed; agents cannot operate freelance and must sit under a licensed brokerage, so a personal-brand site must still carry the brokerage identity. PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021) requires consent-based processing and explicit opt-in for marketing, but its executive regulations were reportedly still unpublished as of mid-2026. All of this is from secondary sources (brokerage/consultancy blogs, law-firm summaries), not primary DLD/RERA text.

### Cited Findings
- Trakheesi permit required for every property listing on your own site, not just portals; brokerage fee AED 1,000 + AED 20 knowledge fee per advert (individual owner AED 220); QR code mandatory on every property advert since April 2023; broker BRN and agency ORN must appear on each advert; off-plan requires developer and DLD permit numbers and escrow details (26 Jun 2026; the article calls the QR "Madmoun") — [Skimbox](https://www.skimbox.co/en/resources/blogs/real-estate-website-development-dubai)
- Permit number is "required on every advert, online included"; advert must match the permit; advertising an off-plan project/unit "generally requires a no-objection certificate (NOC) from the developer"; DLD fines "starting from AED 50,000", plus listing removal or licence suspension; Abu Dhabi has a separate Madhmoun permit via ADREC/DARI (guide dated 12 Jun 2026) — [PropSpace permits guide](https://www.propspace.com/blog/uae-real-estate-advertising-permits)
- Permit scope described as covering social media posts, WhatsApp marketing, print and "even your own website"; a personal social account does not exempt a post that functions as property advertising; on social, the number must be in the creative or caption, not only a bio link (secondary guides) — [Driven Properties](https://www.drivenproperties.com/blog/trakheesi-permit-dubai-guide), [Groovy Web Trakheesi guide](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/trakheesi-permit-dubai-brokers)
- Required display items per a 2026 exam-prep guide: QR code, brokerage registered trade name + ORN, listing broker name + BRN, Trakheesi permit number — [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ae-dubai-real-estate-broker-exam/ch03-brokerage-operations-compliance/ch03-sec02)
- Permit is issued against a specific Form A (owner listing authorisation) and cannot outlast it; permits are issued to RERA-registered entities — [Real Estate Club Dubai](https://realestateclubdubai.com/blog/property-management/trakheesi-permit-in-dubai-what-it-is-how-to-get-it-and-why-short-term-landlords-need-one), [Oliva](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/trakheesi-permit-dubai-listings-guide)
- Fine conflict: one source says AED 50,000 first offence; another AED 50,000 per listing; neither confirmed against official text — [Oliva](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/dubai-real-estate-brokerage-regulations-2026), [PropSpace permits guide](https://www.propspace.com/blog/uae-real-estate-advertising-permits)
- "Just Sold" style ads also subject to stricter rules (Gulf News, Dec 2024 — older) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/dubai-brokers-will-need-to-follow-new-strict-rule-on-just-sold-ads-1.1734060989359)
- Freelancing: RERA does not recognise freelance agents (2010 statement — old, but repeated in 2026 guides); every RERA-certified agent's broker card is tied to a registered brokerage; operating outside one is illegal with fines cited at AED 50,000+ (brokerage-blog figure, UNVERIFIED) — [Emirates 24/7, 2010](https://www.emirates247.com/eb247/companies-markets/real-estate/rera-gets-tough-on-freelance-agents-2010-07-04-1.262523), [Oliva](https://joinoliva.com/en/learn/blog/dubai-real-estate-brokerage-regulations-2026)
- A law-firm brief on brokers' responsibilities and code of ethics exists (Afridi & Angell, Jan 2026; not read in full) — [Afridi & Angell](https://afridi-angell.com/responsibilities-and-code-of-ethics-for-real-estate-brokers-in-dubai/)
- PDPL: consent is the primary default legal basis; use of personal data for marketing requires prior explicit consent with easy opt-out; data subjects may object to direct-marketing processing — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1229090/compliance-communications-with-customers), [ConsentStack](https://consentstack.io/regulations/uae-pdpl)
- PDPL executive regulations: "as of May 2026, formal executive regulations have not been published as a discrete instrument in the UAE Official Gazette"; conflicting decree numbers circulate and quoted fine figures are "not traceable to a primary instrument" — [UpperSetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/uae-personal-data-protection-law-pdpl-the-complete-business), [BusinessDubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/uae-pdpl-data-protection)
- Reported enforcement themes: unsolicited marketing messages, weak controls, unlawful collection (secondary) — [UAE Ahead](https://uaeahead.com/uae-data-protection-law-compliance-obligations/)

### Inferences
- Product requirements implied for EstateSite if it shows specific properties/projects: per-listing fields for Trakheesi permit number + QR code; site-wide brokerage trade name, ORN, agent BRN; for off-plan pages, developer NOC / project permit and escrow references. A site that is only bio + generic area/project content + chat is lower-risk than one advertising specific units, but the line is the regulator's to draw.
- "Personal brand separate from brokerage" is not available legally: the site can lead with the agent's name, but must show the brokerage affiliation; and if the agent changes brokerage, ORN/permits change — the product should make that a simple settings change. Brokerages may also restrict agents' own sites by policy (not evidenced).
- The AI concierge creates specific exposure: (a) if it describes specific units/prices, its output is arguably advertising and must stay consistent with permitted adverts; (b) it collects name/phone/budget, so a consent notice before capture, a privacy policy naming the agent/brokerage as controller, opt-in for WhatsApp follow-up, and deletion/export tooling are prudent under PDPL; (c) chat transcripts are processed by a foreign LLM provider, which raises PDPL cross-border-transfer questions. For the hosted SaaS, EstateSite is a processor; for the self-hosted copy the client carries more of that burden.

### Gaps
- No primary DLD/RERA text was retrieved; whether a personal agent website needs its own DLD registration/approval, and exactly how the rule applies to generic off-plan project pages (vs unit listings), is unconfirmed. Needs a compliance check with DLD/RERA or a UAE real-estate lawyer.
- PDPL: no guidance specific to AI chatbots, WhatsApp lead capture or real estate was found; cross-border transfer rules and status of the UAE Data Office were not retrieved (the Chambers 2026 guide redirected and was not read).
- DIFC/ADGM data-protection regimes and Abu Dhabi/Sharjah advertising rules beyond the Madhmoun mention: not researched.

## 6. Is Arabic / Russian / Chinese multilingual support expected for the buyer audience?

### Takeaway
English is the working language of Dubai property sales, but local web agencies treat Arabic + English with true RTL as table stakes and note that many Dubai sites add Russian; even the AED 299 agent-page product includes Arabic in its paid tier. Buyer-nationality evidence (weak sources) points to India and the UK at the top, with Russia declining but still top-five and China rebounding — and Chinese buyers transact mainly through agent networks rather than web search.

### Cited Findings
- Agency guidance: bilingual Arabic/English with real right-to-left design "from day one"; bolted-on machine-translated Arabic "reads as broken to native speakers"; "many Dubai sites now add Russian" (26 Jun 2026) — [Skimbox](https://www.skimbox.co/en/resources/blogs/real-estate-website-development-dubai)
- knowme.ae Professional tier includes an English and Arabic page (/ar) — [knowme.ae](https://knowme.ae/real-estate-agent-website-dubai)
- Reelly lets agents change presentation language and currency — [Reelly](https://reelly.io/)
- WhatsApp vendors market "Arabic-English auto-translate" for UAE real estate; a brokerage stack write-up notes Dubai conversations mix Arabic and English, which generic bots handle poorly — [go4whatsup](https://go4whatsup.com/uae/whatsapp-for-real-estate/), [Omid Saffari](https://omidsaffari.com/blog/dubai-brokerage-whatsapp-ai-lead-response-stack-playbook)
- Overseas search interest in Dubai property (fam Properties study; search traffic, not purchases): India 20.59%, UK 13.26%, Egypt third; Russia 12th at 2.50%; China outside the top ten, attributed to Chinese buyers transacting via agent networks, developer relationships and referrals — [fam Properties](https://famproperties.com/blog/india-leads-global-interest-in-dubai-property), [Gulf Business 2026](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/real-estate/india-uk-and-egypt-dominate-dubai-property-search-rankings/)

### Inferences
- English-only is workable for an MVP aimed at agents selling to Indian, British and other English-reading investors (the two largest groups), but lack of Arabic/RTL will read as a missing basic to local buyers and to agents comparing against agency quotes that bundle Arabic; Russian is the next most-requested site language; Chinese matters less for a website because that segment does not arrive via web search.
- The AI concierge can answer in the buyer's language even if the site chrome is English-only — a cheap partial mitigation worth stating in positioning, provided the agent's inventory/FAQ content is translatable on the fly and the handoff message to WhatsApp is in a language the agent reads.
